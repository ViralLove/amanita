# Amanita Protocol — Networking Event Cheat Sheet

## 0) The Tone

Your edge is not hype.  
Your edge is: **clear architecture + honest constraints + credible execution**.

Use this posture:

- precise, calm, direct
- never oversell
- never hide trade-offs
- always separate: **what is live**, **what is implemented but not deployed**, **what is planned**

---

## 1) 15-Second Intro (Hallway Version)

> "I’m building Amanita Protocol — a trust and reputation layer for social commerce.  
> We combine invite-graph identity, Soulbound-style reputation, and behavior-based reward flows, so communities can coordinate value with less Sybil noise and more accountability."

---

## 2) 45-Second Intro (Booth/First Conversation)

> "Amanita Protocol is a Web3 social-commerce protocol focused on trust formation, not just token distribution.  
> At the core, we use invite-graph relationships, identity/recovery primitives, and on-chain behavior signals to structure who can participate, validate, and earn.  
> We’re careful about abuse surfaces: circle validation, replay protection, role boundaries, and progressive policy tuning.  
> The goal is practical: better quality reputation and reward signals for real communities, not vanity metrics."

---

## 3) 2-Minute Story (For Serious Interest)

### Problem

- Most social/reward systems are easy to game.
- Identity is shallow; trust is implicit; incentives attract spam.
- Teams often optimize growth before trust quality.

### What Amanita does

- Builds a **trust topology** (invite graph + role logic).
- Uses **identity/recovery and reputation primitives** (SBT-like semantics where needed).
- Links **social validation events** to emission logic with anti-abuse constraints.
- Keeps governance and reward activation tied to behavior quality, not pure activity volume.

### Why this matters

- Better signal quality.
- Higher resistance to coordinated low-quality farming.
- Clearer path from social reputation to economic participation.

### What we do *not* claim

- "Sybil-proof forever" (nobody can promise that).
- "One mechanism solves everything."
- "Decentralization by marketing."

We claim:

- explicit model,
- explicit constraints,
- iterative hardening with tests and policy controls.

## 4) High-Class, Honest Positioning Lines

Use lines like:

- "We optimize for **credible trust**, not maximum noise."
- "Our protocol design is opinionated about abuse resistance."
- "If a rule can’t be explained clearly, it probably shouldn’t be in production."
- "We prefer transparent limitations over fake certainty."
- "We build mechanisms that are governable and testable, not mystical."

---

## 5) What To Say When Asked “So What Exactly Are You Building?”

> "A protocol where social validation has structure.  
> Invite-graph context defines proximity and trust lanes; identity/recovery secures continuity; reward logic consumes validated behavior, not raw clicks.  
> It’s a composable trust-and-incentive layer for real communities."

If they ask for simpler:

> "We’re making reputation and rewards harder to fake and easier to reason about."

---

## 6) “No Bullshit” Status Framing

Always separate three buckets:

### Live now

- "Here is what is already running/usable."

### Implemented, pending deployment/integration

- "Here is what is coded and tested, but not yet fully rolled out."

### Planned

- "Here is our next layer, with known risks and assumptions."

This framing makes you look senior and trustworthy.

---

## 7) Tough Questions — Strong Answers

### Q: "How is this different from standard social tokens?"

> "Most tokenized social systems reward visible activity.  
> We focus on **quality gates**: relationship topology, validation constraints, and anti-abuse checks before reward settlement."

### Q: "Can this still be gamed?"

> "Yes, every system can be attacked.  
> Our approach is layered mitigation: constrain attack surface, monitor behavior, and adjust policy parameters without pretending perfect immunity."

### Q: "Why on-chain?"

> "Because incentives, role transitions, and key trust events should be auditable and composable.  
> Not everything belongs on-chain, but the credibility-critical parts do."

### Q: "Is this too complex for users?"

> "Protocol complexity is acceptable if product UX hides it well.  
> We design mechanism-level rigor and keep user-facing flows simple."

---

## 8) Your Personal Founder/Builder Positioning

Use:

> "I’m not building a meme economy.  
> I’m building a trust architecture where incentives follow validated relationships."

And:

> "I care about mechanism integrity first, growth second — because fake growth dies in production."

---

## 9) 5 Things To Avoid Saying

- "This is fully Sybil-proof."
- "It cannot be exploited."
- "We are fully decentralized already." (if not true)
- "Token will solve adoption." 
- "We’re basically [famous protocol] but better."

Instead:

- define your specific mechanism choices and why.

---

## 10) Conversation Flow Template (Practical)

1. **Context**: "What kind of communities/use cases are you focused on?"
2. **Bridge**: "Interesting — we’re solving trust quality in that exact direction."
3. **Protocol core**: 2-3 mechanism points.
4. **Honest constraints**: one current limitation and how you handle it.
5. **Ask**: "Want to see architecture notes / test strategy / integration surface?"

This moves you from pitch to peer-level technical conversation.

- "We build systems that can be explained under pressure."
- "Our standard is simple: no hidden magic in production logic."
